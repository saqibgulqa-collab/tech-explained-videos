Description:
A chip with tens of billions of transistors starts as ordinary sand. In this video we follow the whole journey: purifying silicon to nine nines, growing a single giant crystal, slicing it into wafers, printing patterns with extreme ultraviolet light from exploding tin droplets, and building up to around a hundred layers over three to four months in rooms cleaner than an operating theatre.

Key figures (checked November 2026):
- Electronic-grade silicon: 99.9999999% pure (nine nines); metallurgical grade about 99% (UniversityWafer)
- Czochralski growth: silicon melted at about 1,400 C, single-crystal ingot pulled from a seed (UniversityWafer)
- 300 mm wafers; EUV light at 13.5 nm; tin droplets about 25 micrometres, laser-to-plasma cycle more than 50,000 times a second; mirrors with 100+ molybdenum/silicon layers reflect about 70%; wafer stage positions to better than 1 nm; about 30 billion transistors on a GPU (Gigazine on Branch Education)
- EUV machine: more than USD 150 million, ASML is the sole producer (Evertiq)
- Up to about 100 layer cycles, three to four months, ISO class 1 cleanrooms (WION)
