You tap send and a grey tick appears in a blink. In that moment your phone locks the message with a brand new key, sends it through an encrypted tunnel to WhatsApp's servers, and the server passes on a box it cannot open. In this video we follow one message: the keys your phone makes when you install WhatsApp, how two phones agree on a secret even when one is offline, why every message gets a new key, what the grey and blue ticks really mean, how photos and group chats are handled, and how long the server keeps your message.

Key figures (checked November 2026):
- About 100 billion messages a day (Mark Zuckerberg, Facebook Q3 2020 earnings call)
- More than 3 billion monthly users (Mark Zuckerberg, Meta Q1 2025 earnings call)
- Keys: identity key, signed pre key and one-time pre keys (Curve25519); only public halves are uploaded (WhatsApp Security Whitepaper)
- Session setup combines four key exchanges into a master secret; works while the recipient is offline
- Each message key is 80 bytes: 32-byte AES-256 key, 32-byte HMAC-SHA256 key, 16-byte IV; keys ratchet forward after every message
- Media: encrypted with a random key, uploaded as an encrypted blob; the key travels inside the message
- Groups: sender keys; message encrypted once, server fans it out
- Delivered messages are deleted from the servers; undelivered ones are kept encrypted for up to 30 days (WhatsApp Privacy Policy)
